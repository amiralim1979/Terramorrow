How your contribution is recorded

Every trip is documented. The Impact Passport is the next level — for sustained contribution over time.

Trip certificate

After each participation, you receive a certificate for that trip — project, dates, and your role. Visible in your profile from your first experience.

Participation history

Even with one trip, your profile shows your contribution history. Build hours across projects and seasons.

Impact Passport

Issued when you reach 100+ hours of verified participation within a one-year period. Annual and renewable.

What is the Impact Passport?

The Impact Passport is TerraMorrow’s recognition for sustained contribution. It is not issued after a single trip.

Requirements: at least 100 hours of verified participation across TerraMorrow experiences within a rolling or calendar year, while remaining an active participant in the network.

It is annual and renewable — a living record that you continue to contribute, not a one-off badge.

Your first trips still matter: each one adds a trip certificate and hours toward the Passport. That path is designed to motivate return participation and deeper involvement with real projects.

About TerraMorrow

Our story

The idea for TerraMorrow came from studying how ecological restoration projects around the world are linked to tourism, public participation and alternative funding.

In many places, people were not only visiting nature — they were taking part in conservation, research and restoration as part of their travel. Tourism, in some cases, was becoming a real revenue and engagement channel for environmental work.

Examples ranged from New Zealand eco-sanctuaries, where visitors support restoration, to international research expeditions such as Operation Wallacea, and platforms that already show travellers are willing to pay to join conservation and environmental research.

That raised a simple question: if so many universities and research institutions already run meaningful ecological projects, why is there no dedicated platform that turns suitable projects into structured, bookable experiences for travellers?

Looking closer at universities revealed the missing piece. They often have the projects, scientific expertise, infrastructure and long-term data. What is frequently missing is a clear connection to people who want to travel, learn and contribute.

TerraMorrow was built for that gap. We do not invent new conservation projects. We work with universities and research institutions, help standardize suitable projects into high-quality participation experiences, and open them to travellers — while scientific ownership stays with the project owner.

The Nordics stood out as a strong place to start: deep environmental research, forest and ecosystem expertise, biodiversity and climate work, and a culture of connection with nature. Not as a geographic limit, but as a supply-side foundation from which the model can expand across Europe.

So the idea evolved from “How can travellers take part in restoration?” into a broader platform question: How do we connect university-led environmental projects with people who want to travel, learn and actively help restore ecosystems?

That is the foundation of TerraMorrow. Leave it better than you found it.
